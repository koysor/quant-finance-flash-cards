# Crisis Alpha

**Topic:** Portfolio Theory & Asset Pricing
**Tags:** crisis alpha, managed futures, trend following, tail hedge, negative correlation, convexity
**Created:** 2026-06-07
**Author:** Claude Sonnet 4.6

## Definition

Crisis alpha is the positive return generated by certain strategies — primarily trend-following (managed futures/CTAs) and long-volatility positions — specifically during equity market crises and drawdowns. These strategies are negatively correlated with equities in bear markets, providing natural hedging value to a portfolio that goes beyond simply holding low-risk assets, because they actively profit from the directional moves and volatility spikes that cause equity losses.

## Key Formula

Crisis alpha contribution to a portfolio is measured by the **conditional correlation** and **conditional Sharpe**:

$$\text{Crisis Alpha} = \mathbb{E}[r_{\text{strategy}} \mid r_{\text{equity}} < \text{VaR}_p]$$

A strategy has crisis alpha if this conditional expected return is positive. The crisis alpha **contribution** to portfolio return is:

$$\Delta \text{Portfolio return}_{\text{crisis}} = w_{\text{strategy}} \times \mathbb{E}[r_{\text{strategy}} \mid \text{crisis}]$$

The convexity of crisis alpha (positive return precisely when needed) is captured by the **option-like payoff profile**: small regular losses in calm markets (like paying an option premium), large gains in crises.

## Example

A portfolio of 60% equities and 40% bonds versus a 50/30/20 split adding a managed futures fund:

| Period | 60/40 return | 50/30/20 (incl. CTA) | CTA alone |
|--------|-------------|----------------------|-----------|
| 2008 | −28% | −14% | +18% |
| 2020 Q1 | −18% | −9% | +8% |
| 2022 | −16% | −6% | +25% |
| 2023–24 (calm) | +18% | +14% | −4% |

The CTA underperforms in calm periods (paying the "insurance premium") but generates strong positive returns in each of three severe equity crises, halving portfolio drawdowns.

## Remember

Crisis alpha is valuable not just for its standalone return but for its **portfolio construction impact**: adding 15–20% of a crisis alpha strategy to a 60/40 portfolio reduces the maximum drawdown more than any equivalent allocation to bonds, gold, or cash. The mechanism is momentum and trend-following: when equities sell off sharply, trend-following systems build short equity futures positions that profit from the continued decline. When central banks cut rates during crises, bond futures rally and trend systems hold long bonds. This cross-asset trend diversification means crisis alpha strategies don't depend on any single asset class — they harvest the directional persistence that crises generate across markets. The trade-off is accepting 2–4% annual drag during prolonged low-volatility bull markets.
