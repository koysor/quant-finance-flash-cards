# Hard-to-Borrow Securities

**Topic:** Short Selling
**Tags:** hard-to-borrow, special, securities lending, borrow cost, locate, short squeeze
**Created:** 2026-06-13
**Author:** Claude Sonnet 4.6

---

## Definition

A security is "hard to borrow" (HTB) when supply of lendable shares is scarce relative to short-selling demand; lenders charge a "special" borrow rate substantially above the general collateral (GC) rate, increasing the carry cost of a short position.

## Key Formula

**Short seller's net financing cost:**

$$\text{Net cost} = \text{Borrow fee} - \text{Rebate on cash collateral}$$

If borrow fee $>$ risk-free rate, the net cost is positive (short seller pays net).

**Daily borrow cost drag:** $\frac{\text{Annual fee} \times \text{Position size}}{252}$

## Example

Stock borrow fee $= 20\%$ p.a. (highly special). Short seller posts \$1m cash collateral, earns rebate at SOFR $5\%$. Net carry cost $= 20\% - 5\% = 15\%$ p.a. $= \$150{,}000$/yr before any directional P&L. The short must fall more than $15\%$ annually just to break even on carry.

## Remember

Extreme specialness is itself a contrarian signal — it indicates heavy short interest and increases the probability of a short squeeze, since any upward move forces short sellers to cover (buy), driving the price further up. GameStop (January 2021) had borrow fees exceeding $30\%$ p.a. and short interest above $140\%$ of the float immediately before its squeeze. A successful short thesis must also be a good *timing* thesis because carry costs are relentless.
