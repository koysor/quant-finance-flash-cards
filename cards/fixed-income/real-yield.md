# Real Yield

## Definition

The **real yield** is the yield on an inflation-linked bond — a Treasury Inflation-Protected Security (TIPS) in the US or an index-linked gilt in the UK. It measures the return an investor earns above the rate of inflation, stripped of all nominal inflation premium.

## Key Formula

From the Fisher equation, the real yield $r_{\text{real}}$ satisfies:

$$r_{\text{nom}} \approx r_{\text{real}} + \pi^*$$

$$\Rightarrow \quad r_{\text{real}} = r_{\text{nom}} - \pi^*$$

where $r_{\text{nom}}$ is the nominal Treasury yield and $\pi^*$ is the break-even inflation rate:

$$\pi^* = r_{\text{nom}} - r_{\text{real}}$$

A **negative real yield** occurs when $r_{\text{real}} < 0$: the investor accepts a return below inflation in exchange for certainty — effectively paying a premium for inflation protection.

## Example

In January 2022, the 10-year US Treasury yielded 1.65% whilst the 10-year TIPS yielded $-0.80\%$. The break-even inflation rate was:

$$\pi^* = 1.65\% - (-0.80\%) = 2.45\%$$

An investor holding the TIPS would earn $-0.80\%$ in real terms — guaranteed to lose purchasing power — but would be fully protected if actual inflation exceeded 2.45% over the decade, which it subsequently did.

## Remember

Real yields are the most direct market signal of monetary policy tightness. The Fed tightens in **real** terms only when it raises nominal rates faster than break-even inflation rises — a distinction central bankers emphasise when communicating policy intent. During 2022, the Fed's aggressive rate hikes drove the 10-year real yield from $-1\%$ to over $+1.5\%$ in under a year: an unprecedented real tightening that repriced every asset class simultaneously, since real yields are the discount rate for all future cash flows.
