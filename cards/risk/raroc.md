# RAROC

## Definition

Risk-Adjusted Return on Capital (RAROC) measures the net return of a business unit or trade relative to the economic capital it consumes, enabling fair comparison across activities with different risk profiles.

## Key Formula

$$\text{RAROC} = \frac{\text{Net Revenue} - \text{Expected Loss} - \text{Operating Costs} + \text{Income on EC}}{\text{Economic Capital}}$$

where Economic Capital (EC) $= \text{VaR}$ or CVaR at the bank's chosen confidence level (e.g. 99.97% for AA-rated institution).

**Value creation condition:** RAROC $>$ hurdle rate (cost of equity, typically 10–15%).

## Example

Trading desk: net revenue $= \$20$m, expected loss $= \$2$m, costs $= \$5$m, EC $= \$100$m, income on EC $= \$5$m (EC invested at risk-free rate). RAROC $= (20 - 2 - 5 + 5)/100 = 18\%$. With hurdle $= 12\%$, desk creates shareholder value.

## Remember

RAROC was pioneered at Bankers Trust in the 1970s to stop businesses maximising return without regard to risk. It aligns trader incentives: a desk generating \$30m revenue but consuming \$500m of EC (RAROC $= 4.6\%$) destroys value despite large nominal profits. RAROC is the link between risk management and strategic capital allocation — divisions with persistent below-hurdle RAROC should shrink or exit.
