# Credit Spread Risk in the Banking Book (CSRBB)

**Topic:** Banking Regulation
**Tags:** CSRBB, credit spread, banking book, liquidity buffer, bonds, IRRBB
**Created:** 2026-10-18
**Author:** Claude Sonnet 5.5

## Definition

**Credit spread risk in the banking book (CSRBB)** is the risk that changes in the market price of credit risk, that is, in credit spreads, reduce the value or earnings of banking-book instruments. It is distinct from IRRBB, which concerns the risk-free rate, and from expected credit losses, which concern a change in the borrower's own creditworthiness; CSRBB captures spread moves driven by market liquidity and risk appetite.

## Key Formula

For a bond portfolio with spread duration $D_s$ and market value $V$:

$$\Delta V \approx -D_s \times V \times \Delta s$$

where $\Delta s$ is the change in the credit spread. The same shift also feeds into net interest income if the instruments are marked to market or refinanced at wider spreads.

## Example

A bank holds a £3bn portfolio of corporate and covered bonds in its liquidity buffer, with a spread duration of 4 years. If spreads widen by 75 bp:

$$\Delta V \approx -4 \times 3\text{bn} \times 0.0075 = -£90\text{m}$$

For positions carried at fair value through other comprehensive income, that loss reduces equity directly, even though no default has occurred and the risk-free curve has not moved.

## Remember

A bank can hedge its interest rate risk perfectly with swaps and still lose heavily when credit spreads widen, because a swap hedges the risk-free leg only. Spread widening tends to coincide with market stress, so CSRBB arrives exactly when liquidity is needed and bonds may have to be sold. Supervisors therefore expect banks to identify, measure and limit it separately from IRRBB.
