# IRRBB Supervisory Outlier Test

## Definition

The **supervisory outlier test** (SOT) compares a bank's largest loss in economic value of equity across the six prescribed shock scenarios with its Tier 1 capital. A bank whose loss exceeds the threshold is flagged as an outlier and attracts closer supervisory attention under Pillar 2. The test is a screening device, not a capital charge.

## Key Formula

$$\frac{\max_k\bigl(-\Delta\text{EVE}_k\bigr)}{\text{Tier 1 capital}} > 15\% \;\Longrightarrow\; \text{outlier}$$

The threshold is **15% of Tier 1 capital**. An outlier may be required to reduce its risk, hold additional capital, or both, at the supervisor's discretion.

## Example

A bank has Tier 1 capital of £5.0bn, so the threshold is $0.15 \times 5.0 = £750\text{m}$. Its worst scenario loses £420m, a ratio of $420/5000 = 8.4\%$: not an outlier. If rates had a worse effect and the worst loss were £900m, the ratio would be $18\%$ and the bank would be flagged.

## Remember

The test uses $\Delta\text{EVE}$ only, so a bank can pass it while still having a vulnerable earnings profile; supervisors therefore also look at the net interest income measure. Because the denominator is Tier 1 capital, the same economic exposure is more worrying for a thinly capitalised bank. Boards use the ratio as an internal limit well below 15% so there is headroom before a supervisor calls it an outlier.
