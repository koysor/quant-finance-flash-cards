# The Trading Book and Banking Book Boundary

**Topic:** Banking Regulation
**Tags:** trading book, banking book, FRTB, boundary, regulatory arbitrage, reclassification
**Created:** 2026-10-18
**Author:** Claude Sonnet 5.5

---

## Definition

The **boundary** between the trading book and the banking book decides which capital rules apply to a position. Trading-book positions, held for short-term resale, market-making or to hedge other trading positions, are charged under the market risk framework (FRTB). Everything else is in the banking book, where credit risk is charged through risk-weighted assets and interest rate risk is covered by IRRBB.

## Key Formula

The boundary determines the regime:

$$\text{Trading book} \;\to\; \text{FRTB market risk capital} \qquad \text{Banking book} \;\to\; \text{credit RWA} + \text{IRRBB}$$

FRTB uses a **presumptive list** of instruments that must be in the trading book (for example positions held with trading intent or in market-making), and a list that must be in the banking book. Moving an instrument across the boundary is tightly restricted and requires supervisory approval, and any resulting capital benefit is removed.

## Example

A bank holds £500m of corporate bonds. If they are in the trading book, they attract a market-risk capital charge based on sensitivities, with no IRRBB. If they are in the banking book held to collect cash flows, they carry a credit risk-weight and their interest rate risk enters the EVE and NII measures. Switching the same bonds from one book to the other changes the capital charge, which is why the rules police it.

## Remember

Before FRTB, banks could lower capital by moving positions that had lost value into whichever book carried the lighter charge. The strict boundary closes that arbitrage, and it explains why IRRBB only covers the banking book: the trading book's interest rate risk is already inside the market risk measure, so counting it twice would be wrong.
